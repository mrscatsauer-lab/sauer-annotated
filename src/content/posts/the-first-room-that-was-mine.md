---
title: The First Room That Was Mine
description: A white iron bed, a floor full of Lego houses, and the first night when nothing happened.

<div class="poem">I remember the white iron bed,
its headboard curved into flowers,
thin metal stems bending
into petals that never opened.</div>

<div class="poem">I remember the day we moved in,
the unfamiliar walls,
the hollow sound of footsteps
in rooms not yet filled with living.</div>

<aside class="note">watch the father. he carries things in. later, he moves through the quiet. that's all he does, and it's everything.</aside>

<div class="poem">And my father,
carrying our things inside.</div>

<div class="poem">I had a bedroom.</div>

<div class="poem">A whole room
with a door that closed,
a bed that belonged to me,
and a floor where I scattered Legos
like pieces of a world
I could finally build myself.</div>

<div class="poem">I made houses there,
small square rooms
with crooked walls
and windows that looked out
onto nothing.</div>

<aside class="note">every lego house gets a bed. that's the whole poem in four lines.</aside>

<div class="poem">I gave them doors.
I gave them roofs.
I made certain
everyone had a place to sleep.</div>

<div class="poem">At night,
the shadows still found me.</div>

<div class="poem">They gathered in corners,
stretched across the ceiling,
and settled beneath the bed
with its white iron flowers.</div>

<div class="poem">I pulled the blankets over my head,
tucked the edges tight around me,
and listened.</div>

<div class="poem">The house creaked.
A car passed outside.
Somewhere down the hall,
my father moved through the quiet.</div>

<aside class="note">you only notice nothing happening when you're used to something happening.</aside>

<div class="poem">And nothing happened.</div>

<div class="poem">No footsteps came rushing.
No voices split the darkness.
The night remained
only night.</div>

<div class="poem">I didn't know then
that safety could be so ordinary.</div>

<div class="poem">I only knew
that beneath those blankets,
with my knees drawn close
and my eyes squeezed shut,
I could fall asleep.</div>

<div class="poem">In the morning,
the sunlight found the iron flowers,
traced their shapes against the wall,
and fell across the little houses
I'd left unfinished on the floor.</div>

<aside class="note">four words. they tell you what the other houses were like.</aside>

<div class="poem">Nothing had been moved.</div>

<div class="poem">Everything was
where I'd left it.</div>

<div class="poem">And the room was still mine.</div>
