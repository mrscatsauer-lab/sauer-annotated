I have mixed feelings about this one, and I've been trying to figure out how to explain them.

In the beginning, I could not put it down. I switched to the audiobook so I could keep going while I cleaned the house. That's how hooked I was. For the first half, this book had me.

Then it didn't.

## The setup

Lark can't speak. She's been silent since she was a child, and that silence is the only thing keeping her alive in a kingdom that hunts people with magic. Then the king comes, and he takes her with him.

The premise is great. The writing is good. Amy Harmon knows how to make a sentence sound like a fairy tale, and I was all in.

## Where it lost me

I love a slow burn. You know this about me by now. So when the book set up a silent girl and a guarded king, I thought, okay, this is going to be *that*. Long looks. Tension. Making me wait.

It wasn't. The romance moved fast, and once it got where it was going, there was nowhere left for it to go. I won't say exactly what happens here (spoilers are at the bottom), but halfway through the book, the part I was reading for was basically over. And there was still about 40% of the book left.

I kept reading, but I wasn't hooked anymore. I was reading to finish it, because I figured something big had to happen at the end. Something did. I still closed the book feeling deflated.

<aside class="note">a slow burn needs the burn. this one just... warmed up.</aside>

## The king

This is going to sound horrible, and I feel bad about it. But I thought Tiras was a little flat.

He could have been so much more. A king with that much on his shoulders should have had more angst, more push and pull, more of a reason for me to worry about whether these two would make it. Instead, they just kind of fell in love.

## So is it romantasy or fantasy?

Somebody needs to settle this, because I'm genuinely asking. If you're selling it as a romance, the love story needs heat. And I don't mean spice. I mean heat: slow burn, tension, angst. This one didn't give me that. If it's a fantasy with a romance in it, then fine, but the fantasy plot wasn't what kept me going either. The relationship was. Until it wasn't.

## What other readers thought

I went looking to see if it was just me. Mostly, it was. A lot of readers love this book. [Smexy Books](https://smexybooks.com/2016/09/review-the-bird-and-the-sword-by-amy-harmon.html) gave it a B+ and called the romance "slow to burn but ends up being romantic and super sweet." [The Library Ladies](https://thelibraryladies.com/2022/04/15/serenas-review-the-bird-and-the-sword) gave it a 9 out of 10, though Serena admitted the two "end up together quickly" and found the bigger plot conflict "the less compelling of the stories." That second part, I agree with.

But I'm not totally alone. Francesca at [Under the Covers](https://archive.underthecoversbookblog.com/the-bird-and-the-sword-by-amy-harmon-2/) gave it four stars and wrote, "At times it felt like insta-love." She said the relationship "jumped from one step to the next quite quickly" and called one of the late plot resolutions "a bit of a rushed, easy fix."

So maybe it's a slow burn if you measure in pages, and a fast one if you measure in feelings. I measure in feelings.

## The verdict

Three and a half stars, and I feel bad even giving that, because this is a conundrum. The writing is good. The first half had me cleaning my whole house just to keep listening. It was a good book, and I liked it. I just wanted the wedding to come later, and I wanted the burn to last.

## Spoiler zone

<aside class="note">last chance. turn back now.</aside>

**Spoilers ahead. If you haven't read it, stop here.**

Here's what actually happened. Lark can't speak, Tiras teaches her to read, and boom, they fall in love. They're married by the halfway point. After that, there's no more slow burn because there's nothing left to burn toward. And then she gets pregnant.

That's the whole romance arc, and it's done by the middle of the book. Everything after the wedding is the fantasy plot, and that's the part I was reading just to finish.
