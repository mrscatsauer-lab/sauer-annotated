<aside class="note">the whole story happens inside one moment of slicing a snack. memory interrupts, the way it really does.</aside>

<mark class="clue">One minute, I am slicing orange cheese and purple grapes for my children, and the next I am six years old,</mark> trying to find a new mother because mine is everything but a mother.

<aside class="note">present tense on purpose. the past isn’t over; it’s happening right now.</aside>

<aside class="note">one small laugh in a heavy room. kids find the funny part.</aside>

Our rough corner of Southern California is riddled with bullet holes and run by gangsters. My step-dad is one of them. Mom and Ramón often go missing for days at a time after drinking too much. I wake up alone on the mattress that sits on the stained shag floor. I look around and grab my Rainbow Brite shirt, a pink tutu, and my favorite jelly shoes. I make a short trip to the kitchen, checking every corner for a monster lurking in the shadows, and then I let out a scream. A large cockroach casually walks out of my cereal box like he hasn’t a worry in the world, <mark class="clue">which he doesn’t.</mark> The roaches have taken up colonies in the kitchen and other parts of the house. <mark class="clue">They’re never evicted.</mark> The shelves of the refrigerator hold half-empty cases of beer and a piece of bologna that is half green.

<aside class="note">the shoes hold all of a child’s faith. one short sentence quietly takes it back.</aside>

With my magical jelly shoes on my feet, I head out in search of food. I know the rules about walking around the neighborhood. If I hear gunshots, I drop to the ground. It has happened a time or two, but both times my magical shoes kept me safe. <mark class="clue">That’s what Mom said, anyway.</mark>

As I pass a fenced yard, I notice a brown-haired lady sitting on a pink blanket, singing to a freckle-nosed baby. Quietly, I squeeze my thin body between the metal slats and walk up to them. The woman looks at my mismatched clothes and bedhead hair and asks if I want to play. After a loud growl from my stomach, she offers me a small container of orange cheese and purple grapes. I don’t hesitate. I grab the container and shovel the food into my mouth. Her eyes double in size as she watches me devour the snack.

<aside class="note">every line before this one builds to this question.</aside>

She asks if I know where my mother is, but I never really know unless she’s passed out on a couch or standing in front of me. I shrug, and then I quietly ask, <mark class="clue">“Do you want to be my mom?”</mark>

<aside class="note">same snack, same small container. now she’s the one handing it over.</aside>

The voice of my toddler brings me back. I look down at my tiny daughter and <mark class="clue">set a small container of orange cheese and purple grapes on her highchair tray.</mark> She looks up at me and excitedly says, “Mama!”

I wipe my eyes and reply, “Yes, baby. I am your Mama.”
