Welcome to **The Teenager Translator**, where I decode what English class is actually asking for, so you can help with homework without needing a teaching degree.

Today's word is the one that shows up on almost every assignment: **analyze**.

## The problem

Here's what happens. The prompt says "analyze." Your kid writes a paragraph explaining what happened in the story. They get it back covered in red ink that says "this is summary." Everyone is confused and someone is crying, possibly you.

<mark>Summary tells what happened. Analysis explains how and why the author made it happen that way.</mark>

## See the difference

Here's an example using *Frankenstein*:

**Summary:** The creature learns to read by secretly watching a family through a crack in the wall.

**Analysis:** Shelley has the creature learn language from outside the house, looking in. Even his education happens as an outsider, which shows readers that he is shut out of human connection before he ever does anything wrong.

The summary is true. It just doesn't *do* anything. The analysis takes a detail and explains what it means.

<aside class="note">if a sentence could be on the back cover of the book, it's summary.</aside>

## The magic question

When your kid is stuck, don't ask "what happened?" They already know what happened. Ask:

**"Why do you think the author did that?"**

Then follow up with **"How do you know?"** That second question is what teachers mean when they say "use evidence." It's the quote or detail that proves the point.

## A simple formula

Most analysis paragraphs come down to three moves:

1. **Say something arguable.** Not "the creature learns to read," but "the creature's education shows he is an outsider."
2. **Prove it with something from the text.** A quote or a specific detail.
3. **Explain how that proof proves it.** This is the part kids skip, and it's the part that earns the points.

## Your homework (it's easy)

Next time you watch a show together, pause on a big moment and ask, "Why do you think the writers did that?" No essay required. You're practicing the exact thinking English class wants, on the couch, with snacks.
