<aside class="note">a Shakespearean sonnet: three quatrains, one couplet. in the first, he’s cast as the hero.</aside>

<div class="poem stanza">From behind a book, he glances my way,
a shy smile, an innocent look.
<mark class="clue">Now</mark> he’s the hero, with lines to say;
<mark class="clue">an opening scene, the perfect hook.</mark></div>

<aside class="note">the book metaphor never breaks. even his charm is borrowed lines.</aside>

<div class="poem stanza">Chapter by chapter, our story grows,
<mark class="clue">stolen lines from dog-eared pages.</mark>
<mark class="clue">Now</mark> he’s charming; it’s all for show.
<mark class="clue">Beneath the binding, something rages.</mark></div>

<aside class="note">each stanza turns on “Now”: the hero, the act, the chaos.</aside>

<div class="poem stanza">The last chapter hurts. He smiles,
teeth bared at last, the monster clear.
<mark class="clue">Now</mark> his books lie in chaotic piles;
<mark class="clue">whole characters just disappear.</mark></div>

<aside class="note">the volta, or turn: twelve lines inside his story, two to show she knows the ending.</aside>

<div class="poem stanza">I’ve read this story before. He doesn’t know
<mark class="clue">I slew another monster long ago.</mark></div>
