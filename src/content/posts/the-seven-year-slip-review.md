My last two nightstand reviews were both three and a half stars. One was slow. One fell flat halfway through. I needed a book that didn't make me work for it.

*The Seven Year Slip* was that book. It's cute, it's an easy, quick read, and I really enjoyed it.

## The setup

Clementine is grieving her aunt, the person who meant the most to her, and she moves into her aunt's old apartment in New York. The apartment has one quirk. Sometimes it slips through time.

Which is how Clementine ends up in her own kitchen with Iwan, a chef who lives there seven years in the past.

<aside class="note">an apartment that slips through time. that's the whole pitch, and it's enough.</aside>

## A plot I hadn't read before

This is what sold me. After a while, romance setups start to blur together. Enemies to lovers. Fake dating. Snowed in at a cabin. You usually know roughly where you are.

I did not know where I was with this one. Two people falling for each other when they're literally seven years apart is a new kind of obstacle. Every time the apartment slips, you don't know if they'll get another moment together or how long it'll last. It gives the romance a tension that doesn't come from anybody being difficult or keeping secrets. It comes from the clock.

There's a line in the book that sums it up:

> It's never a matter of time, but a matter of timing.

That's the whole book. And it's the reason it works.

## What other readers thought

For once, I'm with the crowd. Most reviewers loved it, and most of them loved it for the same reason I did. Bookshelf Fantasies called it "a really satisfying time slip novel" and said it "absolutely delivers." Books, Bones & Buffy said the time slip is just enough magic to keep things interesting without turning into science fiction, and called Poston "simply a genius when it comes to dialog."

The small complaints I saw were fair ones. [Dini Panda Reads](https://dinipandareads.com/2025/07/31/book-review-the-seven-year-slip-by-ashley-poston/) noted a little insta-love and wished there were more of Iwan in the present day. A couple of reviewers pointed out that the whole story is told from Clementine's side, so you only get hints of what Iwan is going through.

## The verdict

Four and a half stars. It's cute, it's quick, and the plot felt genuinely new. Sometimes a book doesn't have to wreck you or teach you anything. Sometimes it just has to be lovely, and this one is.
